ARCHITECTURE 6: Custom CNN Encoder + TransUNet Bottleneck + Attention-Gated Decoder
================================================================================
Three variants tested, all showing classification collapse:

v1 (CLS token classification):     Dice 0.6717, Acc 29.13%, Kappa 0.5782
v2 (warm-started from Arch 4):     Dice 0.6292, Acc 43.69%, Kappa 0.6045
v3 (dual-pathway: CLS + CNN pool): Dice 0.5838, Acc 28.16%, Kappa 0.5638

FINDING: Across all three variants, the transformer bottleneck's
classification pathway consistently collapsed to predicting only 2-3
of the 5 DR grades, regardless of classification head design, warm-
starting, or combining transformer features with CNN-pooled features.
This is consistent with well-documented Vision Transformer literature:
ViTs require either much larger training datasets or large-scale
pretraining (e.g. ImageNet-21k) to learn fine-grained class boundaries
reliably. With only ~680 training images and no transformer pretraining,
this architecture could not match the classification performance of
CNN-based architectures in this project (best: Architecture 5 at
Kappa 0.7557).

Segmentation performance stayed reasonable across all three variants
(Dice 0.58-0.67), since segmentation benefits from direct, dense
pixel-level supervision that classification lacks.

CONCLUSION: For this dataset size and compute budget, a CNN-based
architecture with a pretrained (or well-tuned attention-augmented)
encoder outperforms a transformer-bottleneck architecture for
classification. Architecture 5 (GAN-refined Architecture 3B) remains
the best overall model in this project.


v4 (enhanced encoder: ResBlock + SE attention, deeper capacity): Dice 0.6594, Acc 34.95%, Kappa 0.5377
Best class-spread across all versions (all 5 grades predicted at least once), but aggregate Kappa did not exceed v2.

FINAL CONCLUSION (4 variants tested): Classification performance for this
architecture consistently plateaus at Kappa 0.54-0.60 regardless of
classification head design, warm-starting, or encoder strength. This
replicated ceiling across 4 structurally different fixes is strong,
well-supported evidence that transformer-based classification requires
substantially more data or large-scale pretraining to compete with
CNN-based architectures at this dataset size.
